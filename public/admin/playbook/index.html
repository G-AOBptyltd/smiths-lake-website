📰 News Desk Playbook

For anyone who keeps the Village News page alive. Console: Admin → News desk

Publish a news story (the everyday job)

  1. Open Write a story. Give it a headline, type your story (blank line = new paragraph), check the date.
  2. Press Save & put in feed.
  3. Go to Stories and press Publish to website. The site updates in about 3 minutes.

Share something already on the website

  1. Open Share existing. Filter by section if it helps (History, Services, Groups, Environment, Projects, Emergency).
  2. Click the item, press Add to news feed, then Publish. It joins the feed with today's date and links to its existing page — nothing is retyped or duplicated.

Take a story down (Archive)

  • On Stories, press Archive on the story, then Publish. It comes off the website and the feed completely, but stays saved — the card shows an Archived chip.
  • Changed your mind? Press Restore on the archived story, then Publish, and it's back.
  • Just want it out of the feed but keep the page live? Use the In news feed toggle instead of Archive.

Deleting a story

  • Only a Super Village Admin can delete. Press Delete on a story, confirm, then Publish — it's removed from the website and tidied away in Notion (recoverable from Notion's trash if needed).
  • If you're a regular admin you'll see Request delete instead. Press it and the Super Village Admin gets a "🗑 delete requested" flag on that story to action. (Not sure you need it gone? Archive is the safe, reversible choice.)

Drafts

  • Save as draft keeps a story completely off the website until you're ready. Drafts show an amber chip on the Stories page.

Photos

  • Add a photo right in Write a story: click Photo, choose a picture from your computer, and you'll see a preview. It uploads when you press Save — no Notion needed. Large photos are shrunk automatically.
  • To change a photo later, open the story in Stories → edit, and pick a new file. To swap it for none, that's still done in Notion for now.
  • Stories with a photo get the big picture cards; stories without get a smart coloured panel.
💡 The feed shows newest Publish Date first. Editing an old page never bumps it into the news — only the date and the "In news feed" toggle decide what appears.

🤝 Contributions Playbook

Keep a tidy record of everything the community gives, and celebrate supporters. Console: Admin → Contributions

What this is for

One ledger for every kind of support: money, payments, gifts, time in kind (volunteer hours) and donated services. Residents can also offer support themselves on the public Support Us page.

Log a contribution (the everyday job)

  1. Open Log a contribution. Pick the kind (money, gift, time in kind, donated service…).
  2. Type who gave it, then the amount (dollars) — or hours for time in kind.
  3. Set the status: Received (it's in) or Pledged (promised, not yet arrived). Add a note if useful.
  4. Press Save contribution. It appears instantly in the Ledger — no publishing step.

Pledges from the website

  • When a resident fills in the Pledge or offer form on /contribute, it lands in your Ledger automatically as Pledged, marked "by public form".
  • Follow up using the contact they left, and when it arrives, open the row and change the status to Received.

Fixing a mistake — Archive or Delete

  • Every row in the Ledger now has Archive (or Restore, once archived) and, for admins, Delete — the same pattern as News Desk's Archive/Delete.
  • Archive is the safe, reversible choice: the entry stays in Notion and in the Ledger (shown greyed-out with an Archived chip), but drops out of the totals at the top. Press Restore anytime to bring it back — it returns as Received, so re-check the status if it should be Pledged.
  • Delete is for genuine mistakes (duplicate entries, test data). It's available to a Village Admin or Super Village Admin — Survey Stewards don't see the Delete button. Deleted entries move to Notion's trash (recoverable there if needed) and disappear from the Ledger immediately.
  • There's no publishing step — Archive, Restore and Delete all take effect instantly, same as logging a contribution.

The public Supporters board — opt-in only

  • The fun leaderboard lives at villagefirst.org.au/supporters, with a card and shout-outs on the home page.
  • Nobody is named without permission. A supporter only appears if they tick "thank me publicly" on the form — or, with their say-so, you tick Show publicly on their entry.
  • Add a Display name for how they should appear (e.g. "Jane S." or "Smiths Lake Bakery"). Entries on the board show a 🎉 public badge in the Ledger.
  • The board shows playful tiers (Lake Legend, Champion, Mate, Supporter) and never shows dollar amounts. Everyone else is still counted, anonymously, in the totals.

Taking card donations (money)

  • Online memberships and card donations will run through Tyro on PPCA's own merchant account, and switch on once Tyro enables online (eCommerce) payments. Until then the pledge form is the live way to record support, and the payments card shows "coming soon".
💡 Every pledge is saved to the Ledger. An optional email to PPCA on each new pledge can be switched on later — until then, check the Ledger to see what's come in.

🪪 Membership Playbook

The PPCA member register — from application to financial member. Console: Admin → Membership (Village Admins only)

How members arrive

Residents apply on villagefirst.org.au/membership. The application lands in the Register instantly with an amber Applied chip, and the committee gets a notification email. Nothing to publish, nothing to set up.

The member lifecycle (the everyday job)

  1. Applied — a new application. Press Approve (on the row, or inside the member) once the committee is happy.
  2. Approved — a member awaiting payment. Press Send welcome email — it confirms the approval and explains how to pay.
  3. Paid — press Record payment when the fee arrives: date, method, a reference (bank transfer description or receipt number) and the amount. That makes them financial, and the "Fees collected" total ticks up.
  4. Lapsed — for members who don't renew or have left. Nothing is deleted; the history stays.

Renewals (each new year from 1 July)

  • The membership year runs 1 July – 30 June. Each year is its own row in the register, so past years stay untouched — that's your audit trail for AGMs.
  • Open the Renewals tab: it lists every member from earlier years with no row for the new year. Press Create renewal row (starts them as Approved, unpaid) and/or Send reminder.
  • When their fee arrives, record the payment on the new row as usual.

The AGM / committee export

  • Filter the register to what you need — e.g. Paid + the current year — and press ⬇ CSV. The export matches exactly what's on screen, ready for the AGM or the auditor.

Fixing things

  • Open any member to correct names, addresses, contact details or the membership type — every change records who made it and when.
  • Deleting a register row entirely (typos, duplicates, test entries) is reserved for the Super Village Admin. For real members who've left, use Lapsed.
💡 Member details are personal information — that's why the Membership console is Village Admin only, and why exports should only go to committee members who need them.

🗂️ Projects Playbook

The system of record that ties a project's money, grants, volunteers and reporting together. Console: Admin → Projects

The one idea to understand

A project (e.g. "Recreation Reserve Masterplan") is the spine. Hanging off it: the budget and the co-contribution schedule (shared with the Co-contribution console), the grants being sought for it, and the volunteer groups whose confirmed working-bee hours count as in-kind co-contribution. Open a project to see all of it in one place, and print a committee report.

Create a project and fill it in

  1. Open Projects → + New project. Give it a name (the slug fills in), set the status, and add a description and lead.
  2. In the drill-in, use Budget and Schedule (these open the Co-contribution console for detailed line editing) and set the volunteer hour rate used to value volunteer time.
  3. Link grants from the Grant Portal — pick this project in the grant's "Linked project" dropdown.
  4. Link volunteer groups on the Volunteers tab (or from the Volunteer hub's "Groups & projects" panel). The project then shows those groups' live confirmed hours and their indicative dollar value.

Show a project on the website (Project Hub)

  • Link a project to one or more existing Project Hub cards, or tick "Show as a Project Hub card" and press Create / refresh Hub card. New cards are created as drafts — add photos in Notion, then use Publish website to make them live.

Reports for the committee

  • The Reports tab prints a steering-committee summary (headline numbers plus your editable narrative) and a detailed tracker, and exports the schedule and budget to CSV.
💡 Volunteer hours shown on a project are read live from confirmed working bees and valued at the project's hour rate — an indicative co-contribution figure. Formally pushing those hours into the Contributions ledger is a later step, so nothing is double-counted.

🙋 Volunteer Hub Playbook

Group rosters, working bees and the hours behind grant applications. Group leaders get a simple phone-friendly home at /admin/volunteers/my/ (they land there automatically); Village Admins also have the full console.

The one idea to understand

Every community group has a page ("card") on the website — Landcare, the fire shed, a social group. The Volunteer hub organises everything per card: who volunteers for it, what activities it ran, and how many hours went in. A Community Steward is a volunteer trusted to run one or more cards — they sign in to this same admin portal and see only their groups.

How volunteers arrive

  • Group pages on the website (Environment, Groups & Activities, Emergency sections) carry a "Volunteer for this group" form. Signups land in the Roster as Applied, and the group's steward gets an email.
  • Press Approve to make them Active. Someone volunteering for a second group is recognised by their email — the new group is simply added to their record.

Sign someone up on the spot (QR code)

  • On your group home there's a QR code for your group. Someone says "can I join?" — point their phone camera at it and they land straight on your group's sign-up form. Print the "Join us" poster for the noticeboard, or use Copy link / Text / WhatsApp to share it.
  • The roster shows each volunteer's hours, whether they're a member, and buttons to Call / Email / Text or Say thanks. Milestone badges (10/25/50/100 hrs) and a gentle hours leaderboard celebrate the regulars.

Recording a working bee (the everyday job)

  1. Open Activities → New activity. Name it ("March working bee — foreshore weeding"), pick the group and date.
  2. Add who came and their hours — start typing a name and roster members are suggested; people not on the roster can be typed in free.
  3. Save keeps it as a Draft. Press Confirm when the numbers are right.

Why the hours matter (grants)

  • Confirmed hours are the village's in-kind co-contribution evidence for grant applications — every claimed hour traceable to a named session, a date and a group.
  • In the next stage, a Village Admin will push confirmed activities into the Contributions portal as time-in-kind entries; a pushed activity locks so the financial record can't drift.

Appointing a Community Steward (Village Admins)

  1. Open the Stewards tab, enter their email, tick the card(s) they'll run, press Appoint steward.
  2. If they don't have a sign-in yet, an invitation email goes out automatically and their access is ready the moment they accept. No Netlify, no technical steps.
  3. To change their groups later: Edit cards. To take access away: Remove — they keep their sign-in but see no groups.
💡 Stewards can do everything in this playbook for their own cards — approve volunteers, record activities, confirm hours. They can't see other groups, membership records or village-wide admin.

🏛️ Facility Bookings Playbook

The hall booking book. Console: Admin → Facility bookings (Village Admins). Public page: /facilities/

How requests arrive

Residents see the rates and a two-month availability calendar on villagefirst.org.au/facilities and send a request. It lands in the Booking book as Requested and the committee gets an email — with a ⚠️ flag if the times overlap something already in the book. Nothing is ever auto-confirmed.

Confirming a booking (the everyday job)

  1. Open the request. Check the ⚠️ clash flag and the purpose.
  2. Set the fee quoted if it differs from the standard rates (the bond pre-fills from the facility).
  3. Press Confirm booking, then Send confirmation email — it carries the fee, bond, bank details and the conditions of hire.

Money & the bond

  • When the transfer arrives, record it under Fee, bond & payment — date, amount, bank reference.
  • After the event and the hall inspection, tick Bond returned. The yearly "hire fees received" total sits at the top of the book.
  • Online card payment switches on with Tyro later — this book doesn't change.

Declining, cancelling, completing

  • Decline requests that don't work and send the decline email — it invites them to reply and find another time.
  • Cancelled is for confirmed bookings that fall through; Completed once keys are back and the hall's checked. Nothing is deleted — the book is the record.

Rates & conditions

Edit them under Facilities & rates — the public page updates instantly, no publish step. The conditions text also rides along in every confirmation email.

🎟️ Events Playbook

One-off community events — trivia nights, markets, meetings. Console: Admin → Events. Public page: /events/

Creating an event

  1. New event → name, description, date and time, location, capacity (leave blank for unlimited) and the price as plain words ("$15, kids free") — money is collected at the door for now.
  2. Save keeps it as a Draft nobody can see. Publish puts it on villagefirst.org.au/events instantly — no publish-website step needed.

Registrations, capacity & the waitlist

  • People register on the website in parties of up to 10 and get an automatic confirmation email. The committee gets a heads-up email per registration.
  • When the event fills, new registrations go to the waitlist — and they're told so honestly on the spot. If places free up, press Promote on a waitlisted party; the tool re-checks capacity so you can't accidentally oversell.

Running the door

  • Open the event on your phone on the night. Tick ✓ Arrived as parties come in, and press $ door to record what each party paid — the night reconciles itself.
  • Prefer paper? ⬇ Door list CSV gives you a printable list.

Closing, cancelling, finishing

  • Close registrations stops new signups but keeps the event listed. Cancel pulls it from the website — download the CSV first and email attendees from your usual email.
  • After the night, Mark completed. Door takings show in the console's yearly total.
💡 Online ticket sales (card payment at registration) switch on when the village's own Tyro merchant goes live — the events book stays exactly the same.

🏪 Services Playbook

The village services directory, kept accurate by the people who run each service. Console: Admin → Services. Public pages: /services/

Updating a listing (the everyday job)

  1. Open the service, fix what's changed — description, opening hours, address, website/Facebook, contact details.
  2. Press Save & publish website — the live site updates in about 3 minutes. Plain Save stores changes without publishing (drafting).

Service Stewards

  • Hand each listing to the person who runs the service: Service stewards tab → email + tick their service(s) → Appoint. They sign in and see only their listings; invitations and access are automatic.
  • The same person can also steward volunteer groups in the Volunteer hub — one sign-in, and each console shows them only what's theirs.

What stays with the committee

  • Renaming a service or adding/removing listings (a rename changes the page's web address) — those are done by village admins in Notion, then Publish.
  • The Show contact details publicly tick controls whether the contact person/email/phone appear on the public page.

📣 Advertising Playbook

Local business placements & sponsorships. Console: Admin → Advertising (Village Admins)

Selling a placement

  1. Agree the deal with the business — tier (Major Sponsor / Sponsor / Supporter), the dates it runs, and the fee. Tiers and pricing are entirely the committee's to set.
  2. New advertiser → record the business, a one-sentence public blurb, their website, contact and the deal. It starts as a Draft.
  3. Press Activate when it's confirmed. A placement only shows publicly while it's Active and inside its dates — and it drops off automatically when the end date passes.

Money

  • Record the payment (date, amount, bank reference) when it lands — the yearly advertising income total sits at the top of the console. Card payment arrives with the village's own Tyro merchant later.

Where the ads appear

  • The Public preview tab shows the sponsor strip exactly as it will render, fed by the live placements. It is not on the public website yet — where it appears (homepage, news page, services page) is a committee decision, and switching a location on is a one-line change once decided.

Renewals & endings

  • To renew, extend the dates (and record the new fee/payment). Mark finished placements Expired to tidy the list; Cancelled for deals that fell through. Nothing is deleted — the register is the record.

🌐 Publishing Playbook

How villagefirst.org.au actually updates. Console: Admin → Publish

The one idea to understand

The website is rebuilt from Notion content each time someone presses Publish. Changing things in Notion or the News Desk does not change the live site by itself — pressing Publish does.

When to press it

  • After saving or toggling news stories in the News Desk.
  • After editing website content in Notion (services, groups, history, projects…).

Good to know

  • It's safe — nothing is ever deleted by publishing. It just refreshes the site with the latest content.
  • Updates take about 3 minutes to appear. There's no need to press it twice.
  • The Publish buttons in the News Desk and on the Publish page do the same thing.

👥 Roles & Access Playbook

Who can do what, and how to invite someone. Console: Surveys → Access

The roles

  • Super Admin — everything, every village (Greg / AOB).
  • Village Admin — full control of their village: surveys, news desk, publishing, settings, access.
  • Village Treasurer — the finance roles: Contributions, Co-contribution & budget, and Grants.
  • Village Project Manager (PM) — manages Projects: budgets, schedule, linked grants and volunteer groups, and the steering-committee reports. Money stays read-only — a PM can see the grant-request figure but only a Village Admin can set it, and a PM can't delete.
  • Survey Steward — runs surveys and the News Desk for their village. The right role for most volunteers.
  • Community Steward — a steward whose home is the Volunteer hub: they run the roster, activities and hours for the specific group card(s) a Village Admin assigns them. Appointed from the Volunteer hub's Stewards tab (not the Surveys console) — the invite and access are wired automatically.
  • Committee Viewer — read-only: can see results, can't change anything.

To give someone news publishing (e.g. Rob)

  1. If they're already a Village Admin or Survey Steward — they already have it. Just send them the bookmark: villagefirst.org.au/admin/
  2. For someone new: open Surveys → Access, enter their email, choose Survey Steward (or Village Admin), press Send invite.
  3. They accept the emailed invite, set a password, and sign in at /admin/. Roles take effect on their next sign-in.

Turning modules on or off per role (Super Admin)

  1. Open Admin → Access & roles. The module matrix lists every module down the side and the roles (Village Admin, Treasurer, Project Manager, Steward, Viewer) across the top.
  2. Tick a box to let that role see that module; untick to hide it. Super Admin always sees everything. Changes are per village.
  3. This controls what each role sees — the server still enforces who can actually edit each thing, so hiding a tile is a tidiness/scoping tool, not the security gate.
💡 One login works across every console — Surveys, News Desk, Projects, Publish — and the village picker follows them between consoles.

🌱 Recovery Support Playbook

The weeks after a fire, flood or storm — who needs what, who has offered help, and the record that supports a recovery-funding claim. Console: Admin → Recovery support

The one idea to understand

A recovery event (e.g. "October 2026 east coast low") is the spine. Hanging off it are two registers: the needs (one row per household or site — what they need, how urgent, who is on it) and the offers of help (hands, machinery, a spare room, a trade skill). You match one to the other, and everything your coordinators record along the way becomes the evidence a funding body asks for.

Before anything happens — set it up on Standby

  1. Open Recovery support → + New recovery event. You can create one on Standby before a season, so the register is ready rather than being built in the first frantic week.
  2. Set the volunteer hour rate now. It is what turns recorded hours into a dollar figure later, and it is far easier to agree calmly than mid-recovery. Use the rate your funding body accepts.
  3. Fill in the coordinator — one named person the community and the council can ask for.

When an event happens

  1. Move the event to Active recovery. The start date stamps itself the first time you do.
  2. Log needs as they come in — from a phone call, a doorknock, a recovery hub table. Give each one a priority; Critical sorts to the top of every list.
  3. Log offers the moment someone says "what can I do?". An offer logged is an offer you can find again three days later.
  4. On the Matching tab, pick a need on the left and tick the offers that will cover it. The need moves to Matched and the offer to Allocated automatically.
  5. As work happens, record the hours contributed and the value of donated goods & services on the need, then close it. This is the single most valuable habit — see the tip below.

Privacy — the part that matters most

  • A need holds a real person's name, address and phone, and often why they are vulnerable. It is visible only to this village's Village Admin, Emergency Coordinator and Stewards. It is never published to the website and never shared with another village.
  • Tick Sensitive on a need and the contact block is withheld from Steward-level users by the server — a steward can still coordinate the work without seeing the household's details. Only a Village Admin or the Emergency Coordinator can see or change them.
  • Prefer Withdrawn or Referred over Delete. A recovery record is evidence.

Turning effort into funding evidence

  • The Evidence tab adds up needs logged and closed, people affected, offers delivered, recovery hours valued at your hour rate, and the value of donated goods and services.
  • ⬇ Export evidence (CSV) gives you the summary plus the full needs and offers registers — attach it to a claim.
  • If your village runs recovery working bees through the Volunteer hub, those confirmed hours appear as a cross-check, shown beside the total and deliberately not added to it — counting both would double-count the same work.
  • Money actually received still belongs in the Contributions portal. Nothing here writes into it, so a donation can never be counted twice.
💡 Record hours and values as the work happens, not months later. A contemporaneous record made at the time is exactly what a funding body wants, and it is the difference between a claim that is paid and one that is argued about. Everything the Evidence tab shows is indicative — evidence to support a claim, not an audited figure.

🛍 Pop-Up Store Playbook

A pop-up shopfront for local makers, markets and community fundraising ranges. Console: Admin → Online pop-up store

The one idea to understand — whose money it is

Every seller is paid directly by the buyer, into the seller's own account, through the seller's own payment link or arrangement. The village never holds, pools or forwards a seller's money. So the takings you see here are not village income, the committee is not acting as a payment service, and each seller handles their own refunds. One order always belongs to one seller — that is what keeps settlement clean.

Never enter bank or card details in this console. The only payment information it holds is a seller's own https:// link. The field refuses anything else.

Open a season

  1. A season is one pop-up: a summer market, a Christmas fundraiser, a one-weekend art trail. Open Pop-up store → + New season. It starts as a Draft.
  2. Add the collection points (where buyers pick things up) and the terms buyers see. Say plainly in the terms that each seller is paid directly and handles their own refunds — because they are, and they do.
  3. Set it Open when trading starts and Closed when the season ends. Closing changes nothing about the records: orders stay as a trading history.

Add sellers — and the one gate that matters

  1. + New seller on the Sellers tab. Record who they are, what they sell, and how they take payment.
  2. Work them along Invited → Onboarding → Approved → Live. A seller can only go Live once you have recorded their payment method, their own https payment link where the method needs one, and ticked payment arrangement confirmed.
  3. Until a seller is Live, their products cannot go Active and no orders can be taken. That is the safeguard, and it is enforced on the server — not just hidden in the screen.
  4. To let a seller manage their own listing, put their sign-in email in Seller steward. They then see only their own seller, products and orders — the same way service stewards manage their own service listing.

List a range and take orders

  1. On Products, add each item with a price, stock (or tick unlimited for made-to-order) and how it is collected. Products start as Draft — set them Active to make them sellable.
  2. On Orders, + Take an order for market-day, phone and email orders. Pick the seller, then their items; the total is recalculated on the server from the product prices, so a typo can never change what is owed.
  3. Tell the buyer to pay that seller — the order screen shows the seller's own payment link. When the seller confirms the money arrived, set the order to Paid — confirmed by seller.
  4. Work fulfilment along New → Packed → Ready for pickup → Collected (or Shipped → Completed).
  5. ⬇ Export orders (CSV) gives the committee the season's orders plus per-seller totals.
💡 "Paid — confirmed by seller" means exactly that: the seller told you the money landed in their account. The platform never marks an order paid by itself, because it never receives the money. If a buyer disputes a payment or wants a refund, they deal with the seller — and the season's terms should say so.

🏆 Grant Portal Playbook

Every grant application in one pipeline — research to outcome. Console: Admin → Grant Portal

The one idea to understand

One row per application, not per grant program. If you apply to the same program twice, that is two rows (call the second one "… — round 2"). Each row moves left to right through six statuses, and the portal groups the pipeline by status so you can see at a glance what needs work this week.

The everyday job

  1. + New application. Name it so a reader knows what it is for — "Foreshore accessibility upgrade — round 2" beats "CBP grant".
  2. Fill in the funder and program, the amount requested, and the application due date. The due date is what sorts your pipeline, so always set it.
  3. Pick the linked project — the project this grant would fund. That is what joins the funding story up: the Projects console then shows this grant against that project's budget.
  4. Name an owner. An application with no owner is an application nobody is writing.
  5. Use Notes for eligibility conditions, evidence you still need to gather, and contact names at the funder.

Moving an application along

  • Researching — you are working out whether you are eligible and whether it is worth it.
  • Drafting — you have decided to apply and are writing.
  • Submitted — it is lodged. The submitted date stamps itself the first time you reach this status.
  • Successful / Unsuccessful — the answer arrived. The outcome date stamps itself. On a win, put the real figure in amount awarded — it is often not what you asked for, and the dashboard uses the awarded figure once you have one.
  • Withdrawn — you decided not to proceed. Use this instead of deleting. An unsuccessful or withdrawn application is evidence of effort, and funders and councils do ask.

The four numbers at the top

In progress and being sought cover everything still at Researching, Drafting or Submitted — your live pipeline. Grants won and awarded are your track record. A healthy village usually has two or three applications in progress at any time.

Who can do what

  • Village Admin and Treasurer create and edit applications and change status.
  • Project Manager can read the pipeline (so they can link a grant to a project) but not edit money.
  • Only a Village Admin can delete — and you should almost never need to.
💡 The Grant Portal is in every Village1st plan, deliberately: a village on the smallest plan uses the grant pipeline to win the funding that pays for everything else. If you are on Starter and grants start landing, that is exactly how this is meant to work.

📊 Co-contribution & Budget Playbook

Proving your community's own share of a project — cash and in-kind — and exporting it in a form a council or funder accepts. Console: Admin → Co-contribution & budget

The one idea to understand

Most grants expect the community to contribute something too. That is your co-contribution, and it is rarely all cash — volunteer labour, donated machinery, materials and work you did years ago all count. This console is the grant workbook as a live console: a schedule of what the community is putting in, a budget of what the project costs, and a dashboard that shows the gap.

The four panels

  • Funding dashboard — the headline picture. Start and finish here.
  • Co-contribution schedule — every line of what the community contributes.
  • Project budget — what the project costs, line by line.
  • Project & funding — the project record itself: status (Draft → Active → Submitted → Archived) and the grant being sought.

Adding a co-contribution line

  1. Set the source: Community (your village) or External (someone else's contribution to the same project).
  2. Pick the type — this is the part that matters most:
    • Cash — Prior Investment — money the community already spent on this project before the application.
    • Cash — Fundraising — money you have raised or will raise.
    • WIK — Community Labour / WIK — Volunteer Labour — work in kind, valued at an hourly rate.
    • WIK — Equipment / WIK — Materials — donated machinery time and donated materials, valued at what they would have cost.
  3. Set the confirmation honestly — Yes — actual cost PAID, Yes — completed, Committed, Estimated or To confirm. A funder trusts a schedule that admits which numbers are estimates far more than one where everything is claimed as certain.
  4. Use the reorder arrows to put lines in the order you want them read — the export follows this order.

Reports and export

  • The dashboard shows the total project co-contribution summary — community share, external share, and how it sits against the budget.
  • Download CSV gives you the schedule and budget in a spreadsheet you can attach to an application or hand to a council officer.
  • Archive a line you no longer want counted rather than deleting it, so the history of what you claimed and when survives.

How this joins up with everything else

A project here is the project in the Projects console — same record, two views. Projects adds the grants, the volunteer groups and the committee reports on top. Volunteer hours shown against a project are read live from confirmed working bees and valued at the project's hour rate as an indicative figure; they are not automatically written into this schedule, so nothing gets counted twice.

💡 The single biggest win here is recording in-kind as it happens. A working bee logged the week it happened is co-contribution you can prove; the same working bee remembered eight months later is a guess. Money actually received belongs in the Contributions portal.

📇 Village Profile Playbook

The community resilience profile — what your village has, what it depends on, what it is exposed to, and what it still needs. Console: Admin → Village profile

The one idea to understand

This is the document emergency agencies, councils and resilience funders keep asking for and almost no small community has. Filling it in once — and reviewing it yearly — turns "we think we'd cope" into evidence. It is thirteen sections plus a one-page summary sheet you can hand over.

⚠️ The two privacy tiers — read this before you type

  • Tier 1 is what this form holds: aggregate numbers, facilities, hazard ratings, assets, skills and capability gaps. Nothing that identifies an individual.
  • Tier 2 is held locally by your village and never entered here: named contact lists, who needs help getting out, individual support-network detail. Section 11 is a reference to where that list lives and who holds it — not the list itself.
  • This split is deliberate. It means the profile can be shared with an agency without exposing a single resident, and it is what lets you say yes when someone asks for a copy. If you find yourself typing a person's name and address, stop — that belongs in Tier 2.
  • People and vulnerabilities (section 6) is explicitly aggregate only: how many households, how many likely to need assistance — never who.

Filling it in — the realistic approach

  1. Do not try to finish it in one sitting. Save profile as you go; come back.
  2. Start with what you already know cold: 1 Snapshot, 3 Access, egress and isolation, 7 Community facility capability. Access and isolation is often the most valuable section in the whole document for a small coastal or bush community — how many ways in and out, and what cuts them.
  3. 5 Hazards and risks — rate each hazard your community actually faces. Be honest about the ones you have lived through.
  4. 4 Utilities and infrastructure dependencies — power, water, comms, fuel. What fails first, and what fails because that failed.
  5. 8 Assets and equipment and 9 Community skills — the tractor, the generator, the nurse, the chainsaw tickets. This is what a recovery coordinator will reach for.
  6. 10 Capability gap analysis — the gap between what you have and what you would need. This section is what funding applications quote.
  7. 12 Action register — turn each gap into an action with an owner. A profile with an empty action register is a description; one with a live register is a plan.
  8. 13 Custodianship and review record — who holds it, who you lodged Tier 1 with, and when it is next reviewed. Fill this in or the document quietly goes stale.

The summary sheet

The console generates a community profile summary sheet — snapshot, access and isolation, facilities, top-rated hazards, vulnerability aggregates, key assets and skills, and the contact reference. This is the Tier 1 page you hand to an agency or attach to an application. Verify anything still marked "confirm" before you send it.

How it joins up

The profile is the prepare step. Emergency & Safety is respond. Recovery support is the weeks afterwards — and it draws on the hazards and assets you record here. All three are in every Village1st plan.

💡 Review it once a year, and after anything real happens. The most common failure is not a blank profile — it is a good profile that describes the village as it was four years ago. Set the review date in section 13 and put it in the committee calendar.

🚨 Emergency & Safety Playbook

A standing home for emergency contacts, hours and response information — always there, not buried in a post that scrolls away. Console: Admin → Emergency & Safety

The one idea to understand

Each card is one service or piece of response information on the public Emergency page — the brigade, the police station, the nearest hospital, the evacuation advice. You edit the card's details here, and the public page follows. This is the page residents will go looking for on the worst day of the year, which is why keeping it current is a standing job rather than a project.

The everyday job

  1. Open Emergency & Safety. Every card is listed.
  2. Edit the contacts, hours, address, website and description — and the emergency / response information field, which is where the "what to do" text lives.
  3. Save the card. That updates the record.
  4. Press Publish to website when you are done. The live site updates about three minutes later. Saving alone does not change the public page.

Two things you cannot do here

  • Card titles are not editable. Renaming a card changes its web address, which breaks any link anyone has shared or printed. Ask your super admin if a card genuinely needs renaming.
  • You cannot add or remove cards from this console — that is a content job in the News Desk / Notion. This console is deliberately narrow so the person who keeps emergency info current does not need to learn the whole CMS.

Who does this

The Emergency Coordinator role (<village>:emergency) exists for exactly this job, alongside the Village Admin. It is a good role to give someone who is active in the local brigade or SES and will not otherwise be in the admin console — they get this module and nothing else to worry about.

A sensible rhythm

  • Before each fire or storm season — check every phone number, and check the hours. Numbers change quietly.
  • After any real event — update the response information with what you actually learned.
  • Yearly — read the whole page as a resident would, on a phone, and fix anything that reads like it was written for a committee.
💡 This module sits in the middle of the resilience arc: Village profile is prepare, this is respond, Recovery support is the weeks afterwards. All three are in every plan — no village should be without them.

🏡 Village Pages Playbook

Your About Us, Contact and story pages, editable as plain text. Console: Admin → Village pages

The one idea to understand

Your website's About Us page is built from the story rows listed in this console — your village's "our story" page plus its companions, in the order they are listed. The Contact page comes from the contact row. Edit them here and the website follows.

To change a page

  1. Press Edit on the page you want.
  2. Change the title, summary or full body text.
  3. Save changes.
  4. Press Publish on the Publish website tile. The site updates about three minutes later.

Summary vs page body

If a page has a body, the website shows the body. If it does not, the website shows the summary. So if your edit to the summary is not appearing, the page almost certainly has a body — edit that instead.

Two things to expect

  • Pages laid out with photos cannot be edited as plain text here. Edit those in Notion or through the News Desk, which understands images and rich layout.
  • "Hidden" on a page means it exists but does not render on the website — it is not set to Published / Show on Website in Notion. Ask your super admin if one should be live.
💡 Read your About Us page out loud once a year. Village story pages age faster than anyone expects — they end up describing a committee that has turned over twice and a project that finished years ago.

📧 Subscribers Playbook

The community mailing list — everyone signed up for updates. Console: Admin → Subscribers

The one idea to understand

This console is the view of your mailing list, not the machinery. People sign up through the website, the list lives in Mailchimp, and the two are kept in step automatically. You come here to see who is on the list, who has opted out, and to pull a CSV.

The everyday job

  • The three counters at the top: subscribers, receiving updates, and opted out. The gap between the first two is your opt-outs, and it is normal for it to grow slowly.
  • Filter by Everyone / Receiving updates / Opted out, and by interest where people chose one at signup.
  • Export CSV for a committee paper or an AGM report.

⚠️ Respect the opt-outs

Someone marked opted out has asked not to receive community updates. Do not add them back manually, and do not copy them out of the CSV into a separate email. Under Australian spam rules an unsubscribe has to stick, and a small community notices immediately when it does not.

Sync & maintenance (super admin only)

  • New subscribes and unsubscribes flow in automatically through a Mailchimp webhook. In normal operation you never touch this panel.
  • Check counts compares this list against Mailchimp. Use it if you suspect they have drifted.
  • Re-sync from Mailchimp reconciles the two. It is a repair tool, not a routine step.
💡 The mailing list and the member register are different things, and mixing them up is the usual mistake. A subscriber has asked for news. A member has joined the association and paid a fee. Plenty of people are one and not the other.

📲 App Approvals Playbook

RSVPs, on-site sign-ins and hours coming from the volunteer app. Console: Admin → App approvals

The one idea to understand

Volunteers and stewards use the phone app to RSVP to a working bee, sign in when they arrive, and log their hours. Those hours arrive here unapproved. The value you approve is the value that counts toward a grant — so this screen is where a rough phone entry becomes evidence.

The everyday job

  1. Open Hours to approve after a working bee.
  2. Adjust the hours if they are wrong. The app often records a default (e.g. an automatic 2 hours) — if someone was there for five, change it to five before approving.
  3. Approve. The adjusted figure is what flows through to the volunteer ledger and, from there, into a project's in-kind co-contribution.
  4. Reject anything that is a duplicate or a mistake. Rejections stay visible under Already decided, so nothing vanishes silently.

The other panels

  • On site (last 24 h) — who signed in recently. Useful on the day for a headcount, and afterwards for checking nobody was missed.
  • Coming up — RSVPs — who has said they are coming to the next working bee, so you can plan tools, food and supervision.
  • New joiners — the most recent sign-ups from the app. Worth a welcome message.
  • Already decided — everything you have approved or rejected, filterable. This is your audit trail.

If the panel says "not connected yet"

The volunteer app lives in its own database, and this site needs to be pointed at it. A super admin sets VAPP_SUPABASE_URL and VAPP_SUPABASE_SERVICE_KEY in the site's Netlify environment and redeploys. Until then this console is empty — the app itself still works, and your stewards still see the data in the app.

How it joins up

Approved hours roll into the Volunteer hub, and from there a project values them at its hour rate as indicative in-kind co-contribution. That is why the adjustment step matters: an approved 5 hours at $45 is $225 of provable community contribution, and a careless 2 hours is $135 you cannot claim.

💡 Approve weekly, not quarterly. Volunteers forget how long they were there, and a grant acquittal written from hours approved months later is the single most common place a village's in-kind contribution gets understated.
